# DPrL GPU and reconstructed REASON experiments — 2026-10-09

**Follow-up:** [GPU optimization results](proposal/GPU_RESULTS.md) now provide
stronger matched controls: 14.975 ms for Family and 24.623 ms for WN18RR, averaged
over three batch medians. Expanded WN18RR testing exposed the same upstream
candidate-scatter race as Family, so the follow-up applies the stable correction
to both datasets. The WN18RR results below retain their historical uncorrected
implementation boundary and must not serve as the denominator for the new
speedup claim. Earlier hybrid estimates have not been remapped to the new GPU
pipeline. See the [research proposal](proposal/README.md) for the resulting scope.

The measured bottleneck is excessive dense candidate work plus GPU policy and
environment kernels. The implemented REASON mapping does not outperform the
best validated GPU controls under the tested model assumptions. MNIST gains
substantially from CUDA Graphs; KG remains dominated by work that this REASON
reconstruction leaves on the GPU.

**Evidence boundary:** GPU latencies and counters below are measurements on
Slurm `athena/node2`, NVIDIA RTX A5000 24 GB. REASON latencies are **uncalibrated
model estimates**, added to measured retained GPU work. Functional accelerator
values were executed and checked. There is no REASON hardware measurement,
RTL calibration, or reproduction of the authors' Accel-Sim system.

## Complete batch latency

All numbers are milliseconds per completed batch, warm inference, strict FP32
with TF32 disabled, resident inputs, frozen checkpoint, and matching query
content. KG uses 64 queries and a 20-step proof budget. GPU entries are medians
of 10 native samples or 20 graph samples; MNIST uses 20 samples. Compilation,
dataset loading, checkpoint loading, and result writes are outside this boundary.

| Workload | Batch | Native compiled GPU, measured | GPU with complete CUDA Graph, measured | Best GPU control, measured | GPU + REASON with query tiling, estimated | Hybrid / best-GPU latency |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Family, corrected candidate compaction | 64 | 52.926 | 46.631 | 46.631 | 146.340 | 3.14x slower |
| WN18RR | 64 | 115.063 | 112.247 | 112.247 | 304.312 | 2.71x slower |
| MNIST four-digit PI marginal scoring | 1 | 1.131 | 0.110 | 0.0766 | 0.1038 | 1.36x slower |
| MNIST four-digit PI marginal scoring | 64 | 0.883 | 0.283 | 0.2198 | 0.6023 | 2.74x slower |

MNIST's best control also removes impossible mask terms on the GPU. This is
exact-zero work reduction with numerical parity, not a hardware gain. Its eager
latencies were 2.717 ms (batch 1) and 2.844 ms (batch 64). Best-GPU p95 latencies
were 47.557, 112.569, 0.0842, and 0.2211 ms in the table's order.

The hybrid estimate offloads final policy dot products for KG and the
sum-product recurrence for MNIST. **KG unification, substitutions, history,
candidate generation, MLPs, and normalization remain on the GPU.** A
symbolic-only REASON dispatcher has no implemented KG lowering and falls back
entirely to the GPU. The table therefore measures a concrete supported-operator
offload experiment, not accelerated first-order proof execution.

The full-batch accelerator mapping predicts 208.471 ms for Family, 429.871 ms
for WN18RR, and 1.617 ms for MNIST batch 64. Serial internal query tiles of one
reduce register live ranges and produce the better estimates in the table.
Every arithmetic operation and the complete interface payload are preserved.
These are two compiler mappings of the same assumed hardware, not bounds on
the original REASON implementation.

## Where the GPU time goes

Nsight Systems captured 2,790 kernels for corrected Family and 2,763 for WN18RR
per proof batch. Shares below are fractions of **captured kernel time**.

| Kernel group | Corrected Family | WN18RR | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Large policy SGEMMs | 43.4% | 32.1% | Dense neural policy over the padded candidate tensor |
| Fused add / layer normalization / ReLU | 13.0% | 10.8% | Repeated feature passes and memory traffic |
| Candidate / state compaction kernel | 8.4% | 11.0% | Irregular state construction and movement |
| Original scatter-assignment family | Replaced in corrected compaction | 17.1% | WN18RR's large intermediate candidate arrays |

Separate staged replay, with terminal outputs checked against native inference,
recorded policy spans of 37.01 ms for Family and 57.04 ms for WN18RR; environment
spans were 11.77 and 48.16 ms. Reset added 7.47 and 9.85 ms. These diagnostic
spans include scheduling gaps and instrumentation and do not form an exact
breakdown of the differently fused native path.

Selected Nsight Compute hotspot launches provide the following counter evidence:

| Kernel | Family SM throughput | Family DRAM throughput | WN18RR SM throughput | WN18RR DRAM throughput |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `ampere_sgemm_128x64_tn` | 56.1% | 16.8% | 64.8% | 21.1% |
| Fused layer-normalization path | 35.7% | 87.7% | 33.3% | 91.1% |
| Original scatter-assignment path | 15.8% | 25.7% | 19.8% | 32.4% |

These are means over 5, 4, and 3 selected launches, respectively, not global
utilization averages. Family counters were collected on the untouched-source
diagnostic; its dense policy shapes are unchanged, while its original scatter
path is excluded from the corrected baseline. SGEMM used 122 registers per
thread and reached about 27–30% occupancy in those samples. The normalization
path is bandwidth intensive; scatter's low throughput suggests irregular
access, contention, or latency rather than saturated DRAM alone. These
measurements do not support a single universal "memory-bound symbolic work"
explanation.

Profiler overhead matters: corrected Family's capture window was 75.84 ms but
its summed kernel duration was 48.53 ms; the unprofiled native median was
52.93 ms. WN18RR's corresponding capture/window totals were 122.03/114.74 ms.
Do not equate captured idle gaps with unprofiled GPU idleness. The PyTorch
profiler produced CPU-only traces on this installation despite requesting CUDA
activities; GPU conclusions use Nsight's actual CUPTI records.

## Wasted candidate work and dependency chains

Across all 20 proof steps:

| Measured structural count | Family | WN18RR |
|---|---:|---:|
| Allocated dense candidate slots | 166,400 | 335,360 |
| Valid candidates belonging to active queries | 3,788 | 1,188 |
| Valid active candidates / all allocated slots | 2.28% | 0.354% |
| Active query-lane fraction | 28.28% | 29.22% |
| Valid candidates / active-lane capacity | 8.05% | 1.21% |

The neural policy processes the padded shapes even when a slot is invalid or
a query has finished. The environment also constructs candidates before masking
completed queries. Roughly 71% of query lanes are already inactive when averaged
over the depth budget. This is a measured work-volume mismatch, not a prediction
of a proportional latency improvement: packing, synchronization, and the
remaining environment work also have costs.

Checking `done.all()` on the CPU after every step did not remove steps for these
batches and slowed Family to 59.71 ms and WN18RR to 121.67 ms. Both still needed
the full 20-step batch budget. Removing completed queries or refilling their
lanes is more relevant than inserting a host synchronization each step.

![Candidate and active-lane counts](results/20261009-a5000/analysis/candidate_work.png)

For MNIST, a complete CUDA Graph reduced the same compiled computation from
1.131 to 0.110 ms at batch 1, and from 0.883 to 0.283 ms at batch 64. This is
direct evidence of substantial host/launch overhead. Exact-zero compaction
then reduced graph latency to 0.0766 and 0.2198 ms. A comparison only against
eager or ordinary compiled inference would attribute too much gain to REASON.

## Why this REASON mapping does not help

The model uses the paper-derived configuration and explicit assumptions listed
in [RECONSTRUCTION.md](RECONSTRUCTION.md), based on
[REASON sections V–VII](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20784v1). The reconstruction
imports the pinned public DPU-v2 graph builder and adds binary-DAG lowering,
bounded tree mapping, finite banked registers, spills/reloads, SRAM traffic,
dependencies, output buffering, configuration, and interface costs.

For KG, even a zero-time, zero-transfer accelerator would leave measured GPU
replay costs of 52.13 ms for Family and 114.79 ms for WN18RR under this partition.
Those are already close to native compiled latencies. Offloading a small final
dot product leaves the expensive MLP and symbolic environment intact.

The offload sends 8.42 MB (Family) or 16.90 MB (WN18RR) per step, including
candidate vectors and outputs. Twenty serial invocations make the modeled
interface contribution about 5.30 or 10.60 ms. The query-tiled model adds
88.91 or 178.92 ms of accelerator execution. Retained GPU time plus these costs
produces the table's estimates. The timing model is conservative and has no
shared-L2 contention or multi-batch overlap model.

Mapping sensitivity is large. For MNIST batch 64, full-batch scheduling spills
117,504 register values and predicts 1,390.5 microseconds of accelerator
execution. Internal single-query tiles reduce these to 17,088 spills and
375.8 microseconds. This identifies register allocation and serialized operand
movement as limitations of the **implemented model/compiler**. It does not
establish that the authors' optimized compiler has the same bottleneck.

Functional execution is separate from hardware timing. The CPU interpreter plus
GPU emulation/checking took about 3.00 seconds for Family and 14.21 seconds for
WN18RR; the MNIST arithmetic interpreter took about 3 milliseconds. Those times
are retained for audit and are not used as accelerator latency.

## Follow-up: step-by-step bottleneck audit

Re-reading the retained CUPTI SQLite records identifies **20 batched GEMV dot
kernels totaling only 0.333600 ms for Family and 0.643074 ms for WN18RR**.
These are 0.687% and 0.560% of captured GPU kernel time. They implement the
policy's final `torch.matmul(p_obs, p_act.transpose(-2, -1))`, the operation
selected for offload. They exclude MLPs, normalization, and host dispatch.

The implemented per-round sequence is:

1. GPU embeds the current goal and all padded candidates, runs the shared MLP
   and policy heads, then normalizes their vectors.
2. The modeled interface supplies those vectors to REASON.
3. REASON computes only query/candidate dot products, using internal candidate
   tiles of eight and serial query tiles of one for the best tested mapping.
4. The GPU receives scores, applies scaling/masking, and selects an action.
5. The GPU applies the rule, performs unification and substitutions, constructs
   candidates, prunes history, and updates proof state. That state feeds the
   next round. The tested batch requires all 20 rounds.

The dot kernel removed from the GPU is much smaller than the work left there.
The hybrid estimate can be reconstructed exactly as follows; all values are
milliseconds per 64-query, 20-round batch:

| Component | Family | WN18RR | Evidence |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Retained GPU replay | 52.127758 | 114.791844 | Measured, partitioned replay |
| REASON internal execution | 88.911360 | 178.918400 | Uncalibrated model |
| Interface and handoffs | 5.300800 | 10.601920 | Model, not measured PCIe |
| Sum: hybrid estimate | 146.339918 | 304.312164 | Derived |
| Best complete GPU control | 46.631213 | 112.247348 | Measured, CUDA Graph |

The interface assumes 64 bytes/cycle at 500 MHz and two 1-us handoffs per
invocation. Payloads are 8,417,280 and 16,899,072 bytes per round. Transfer is a
secondary modeled cost here. CPU functional interpretation and actual emulation
round trips do not enter these estimates. DAG lowering/configuration is outside
the warm boundary; the static templates are reused.

The internal model clock is dominated by these issue delays:

| Modeled delay, all 20 rounds | Family ms | WN18RR ms | Share of internal execution |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Operand load / reload from SRAM | 66.47808 | 133.95456 | 74.8–74.9% |
| Register spill writes to SRAM | 19.78368 | 39.64416 | 22.2–22.3% |
| Register-bank read waits | 2.41920 | 4.89472 | 2.72–2.74% |
| Dependency waits | 0.01280 | 0.00256 | Below 0.02% |
| Unattributed pipeline/tail cycles | 0.21760 | 0.42240 | About 0.24% |

For these runs, the nonzero delay categories advance the global issue clock;
their sum plus the residual matches execution cycles. The residual is **not**
pure arithmetic time: arithmetic overlaps those waits. Nor are these fractions
independently removable costs on real hardware. The model's aggregate arithmetic
issue utilization is about 2.34%, not a measured hardware occupancy.

Two implementation assumptions explain this result:

- In `reason_model.py`, the `if loads:` block directly increments a single
  global `clock`. It cannot issue another independent tree block during that
  load delay. Asynchronous prefetch and load/compute overlap are not represented.
- Output placement is `out_bank = pe % c.banks`. With 12 tree PEs, intermediate
  results target banks 0–11 even though the configuration contains 64 banks.
  Per-bank allocation spills when a local bank fills. The eight-candidate tile
  peaks at only 590 live words out of 2,048 aggregate registers, yet spills.
  Level-ordered scheduling and this fixed placement create local pressure.
  These are reconstruction/compiler assumptions requiring validation, not
  established limitations of the paper's bank topology or compiler.

There is no modeled external-DRAM overflow in this mapping. The quoted spills
are register-to-SRAM traffic. Thus the slowdown identifies an unsuitable current
offload partition and a poorly utilized reconstructed schedule; it does not
demonstrate that the original REASON hardware is slower than the GPU.

For the GPU, staged diagnostics show the following sequence. Each per-round
value is the 20-round average, while reset runs once:

| GPU stage | Family ms | WN18RR ms | Scope |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Reset and initial candidates | 7.470112 | 9.853440 | Once per batch |
| Embedding + policy + normalization + dot | 1.850267 | 2.851995 | Per round |
| Action selection | 0.013627 | 0.014848 | Per round |
| Symbolic environment and next candidates | 0.588482 | 2.407987 | Per round |
| State copies | 0.041171 | 0.046234 | Per round |

These independently compiled, instrumented spans must not be added to native
kernel durations or treated as an exact decomposition of the CUDA Graph run.
Native Nsight hotspots above locate the policy cost in dense SGEMM and
normalization; WN18RR additionally spends 19.572 ms in scatter-assignment and
12.596 ms in its main candidate/state-compaction kernel. Candidate generation
runs before the completed-query mask in `EnvVec.step`, so completed lanes still
incur this work. Action selection is not a major bottleneck.

For MNIST batch 64, the analogous total is 0.188963 ms measured GPU prefix +
0.375808 ms modeled recurrence + 0.002976 ms modeled interface + 0.034563 ms
measured GPU suffix = 0.602311 ms. The best complete GPU control is 0.219755 ms.
Its model delays differ from KG: operand loads 36.1%, bank reads 35.3%, spills
27.3%. At batch 1, the separately measured GPU segments already total 0.095868 ms
versus 0.076570 ms for the compact whole-graph control, before accelerator costs.

## Correctness, training, and quality

An upstream reproducibility defect was found before accepting the Family
comparison. Eight identical native calls produced eight terminal hashes;
the first observed state divergence was in candidate construction. The pinned
`pack_results` scatters invalid and overflow entries onto the last valid slot
and assumes ordered writes. Duplicate destinations are explicitly documented as
nondeterministic by [PyTorch 2.9](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.9/generated/torch.Tensor.scatter_.html).

The opt-in stable adapter preserves fact-then-rule ordering, retains the first
K valid candidates, and redirects discarded writes to a separate sink. Both
Family backends use it. Family was then retrained from scratch for 32,768
environment transitions. The untouched upstream result (57.18 ms) is preserved
as a diagnostic, not used for an equal-answer speedup comparison.

Final Family and WN18RR native outputs matched across five additional repeats.
Staged replay, all 20 hybrid action selections, and terminal success/depths
matched. Maximum valid-logit differences were 0.000153 and 0.000183,
respectively, within the checked `atol=rtol=2e-5` relative/absolute criterion.
MNIST maximum log-score differences were 1.35e-7 (batch 1) and 1.13e-6 (batch 64).
All graph controls passed output checks. Thirteen independent CPU tests cover
arithmetic enumeration, dot products, scheduler work/dependencies, query tiling,
watched-literal propagation, and candidate compaction boundaries.

| Checkpoint | Training budget | Quality measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Family, stable adapter | 32,768 environment transitions, 8 PPO rollouts | Sampled MRR 0.6387; Hits@1 0.6250 |
| WN18RR | 32,768 environment transitions, 8 PPO rollouts | Sampled MRR 0.3129; Hits@1 0.28125 |
| MNIST PI | 1,000 Adam updates, batch 64 | Digit accuracy 98.48%; four-digit sum accuracy 88.88% |

KG ranking used 16 test queries, 100 negative corruptions per head/tail side,
and seed 100. It is not exhaustive ranking. Its separately measured complete
ranking latencies were 3.183 seconds for Family and 8.206 seconds for WN18RR.
WN18RR retains the upstream 8,000 fact-pair cap; optional KGE checkpoint fusion
is disabled. These locally trained profiling checkpoints are not established
as converged or equivalent to paper checkpoints. Results cover one training
seed and one frozen proof batch per KG dataset.
Hybrid correctness checks cover those frozen proof batches, not the entire
ranking workload; ranking metrics and ranking latencies are GPU-only results.

MNIST preserves the shipped unconditional carry recurrence and per-digit
marginal log score. It is not exact joint probability of the target sum, and
the public accuracy evaluator uses neural digit argmax rather than this
symbolic scoring path. This experiment deliberately measures the PI scoring
control case.

## Recommended next changes

1. Compact valid candidate rows before the policy MLP and avoid executing
   completed queries. Apply the same work reduction to both backend comparisons.
2. Reduce large temporary candidate tensors, repeated state clones, and
   scatter/gather traffic in GPU unification and history processing. Re-measure
   the normalization and state-construction hotspots after compaction.
3. Retain CUDA Graphs for static GPU segments. Use a device-side work queue or
   batch refilling for uneven proof lengths rather than per-step CPU polling.
4. A DPrL-oriented accelerator extension needs priced support for dynamic
   candidate generation, substitutions/unification, and persistent proof state.
   The existing sum-product-only mapping leaves those bottlenecks in place.
5. Calibrate scheduling, bank topology, interface latency, and memory behavior
   against a hardware reference before making REASON hardware speedup claims.
